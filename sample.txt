The Lantern Keeper

At the edge of the village stood an old lighthouse, and in the lighthouse lived a keeper named Mara. Every evening she climbed the stairs, lit the great lantern, and watched the light sweep across the dark water. The fishermen said that the light was the kindest thing in the whole town, for it told them that home was near.

One winter night, a storm rolled in from the sea. The wind howled, the waves grew tall, and the rain fell in sheets against the glass. Mara saw a small boat far out on the water, tossed about like a leaf. She knew the boy in the boat, because he was the baker's son, and he had gone out to fish alone.

She did not wait. She turned the lantern toward the rocks, and then she took a second lamp and ran down to the shore. She waved the lamp high, again and again, so that the boy could see the path between the rocks. The boat rose and fell, and for a long time the sea seemed to hold its breath.

At last the boat slid onto the sand. The boy jumped out, cold and tired, and Mara wrapped him in her coat. "The light never leaves," she said with a smile, "as long as someone is there to keep it."

In the morning the whole village came to thank her, and the baker brought warm bread. Mara laughed and said that the lantern had done the work, but everyone knew the truth. The light was only as bright as the person who cared for it.
